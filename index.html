Financial and estate planning conversations for Singapore families.

Every family starts with one acorn.

A child is born in Singapore. Nobody hands the parents a plan. I have sat with new parents who had the cot, the pram and the hospital bag ready, and nothing else. The soil is whatever they put in place in the first year, while nobody is looking.

  • Cover on the two people who earn.
  • Someone named to take the child.
  • Money set aside for the family's immediate needs.

The child never sees the roots.

School, piano, the trip to see the grandparents. What pays for it is quiet. It was decided years earlier, at a kitchen table, by two people who hoped never to need it.

  • Care. Who looks after the people.
  • Cash. Money there when it is needed.
  • Control. The right authority over the right asset.
  • Conflict. A family designed not to fight.

As they build a life of their own.

First salary. First CPF statement. The same questions the parents once answered, asked again in their own name. The habit was planted long before the pay slip.

  • Own cover, before anyone depends on them.
  • A floor of savings before the first investment.
  • The first honest conversation about what the money is for.

Then it is your turn to be the shade.

A child of your own. The questions stop being academic. Who has the child tonight if you are both in hospital. What pays the mortgage in month two. Who can act when you cannot sign.

  • It is easy to postpone these questions.
  • Everything above this layer rests on it.

Wealth that outlives the person who built it is built in layers.

Not one document and not one product. Layers, each holding the one above it. I call the design the Stewardship Framework.

The acorn falls. The roots are already there.

The next generation does not start from bare soil. It starts from a plan that was already in place on the day they arrived. Prudent financial planning pays off, most of all for the people who come after you.
